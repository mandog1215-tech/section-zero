UNDER THE FLOORBOARDS

The Engine Lab

No off-the-shelf engine hums under this studio's game. The machinery is written by hand, in C, by the studio — which makes the lab less a department and more a promise: if it moves, the studio can explain exactly why.

Built, not bought

AirportSim runs on a hand-rolled engine: its world — aircraft, contracts, weather, money, memory — is simulated in plain C, kept deliberately separate from the machinery that draws it. That separation is the lab's favourite trick. The world can be run headlessly, thousands of times, by tests that never open a window.

The SDL3 standard

The studio's modern foundation: C11 on SDL3, pinned and carried with the project. AirportSim was moved onto that foundation, retiring its SDL2 underpinnings — and every lesson learned on the bench belongs to the game.

Same seed, same world

Randomness in the lab is a tool with a serial number. Worlds are dealt from seeds, so any scene can be dealt again, exactly, for the re-test — and a check exists to confirm the dealing hasn't drifted. Chance is welcome in the game; it is not welcome in the results.

An engine you wrote yourself can still surprise you — but it can never hide from you.

Currently on the bench

The lab's work is AirportSim: the game is being ported fully to Windows, and its engine rests with the 7.0 test copy, in closed testing. The transplant before this one — AirportSim's move from SDL2 to SDL3 — was completed on 11 November 2026.
