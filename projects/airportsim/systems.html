A · Simulation foundation

Established

Fixed-step, SDL-independent core

Simulation modules never include SDL headers and link into a headless test binary (an explicit architecture decision in the project's own records). One plain-data global state holds the whole world; seeded random streams make runs reproducible, and the random state is saved with the world.

Established

Saves & version migration

Versioned save format with integrity checks and migrations; fixture saves spanning versions 4 through 13 load inside the automated suite, clean under memory-safety instrumentation. The most thoroughly proven system in the game. Detail in section F.

B · Global aviation world

Demonstrated

Airport database & ambient network

4,531 real airports; a seeded ambient network of airlines and fleets keeps the world flying around the player. In the audited session: 1,405 flights airborne on 1,659 routes.

Demonstrated

Weather & world presentation

Live-style weather systems (23 active in the audited session), real METAR reports, country/locale profiles for 232 territories, and the global map with its day/night terminator.

World ⇄ airport. Your airport is a node in the network, not a separate mode: the same flights, weather and clock run through both.

C · Airport operations

Demonstrated

Aircraft & ground operations

A full aircraft state machine — approach, landing, taxi (with pathfinding), stand assignment, turnaround services, departure — at 26 real layouts, with automated or player-operated ATC and ATIS procedures.

Implemented 2026-11-03 — DEF-007 (PR #44)

ATC final speed control

Documented by the original project itself as missing: approach speed control in the final segment. Registered as K7, it was built on 2026-11-03 as the foundation of the ATC rework — the controller now assigns speeds on final (section H, below). Its first rig lane failed on two faults in the new code; both were fixed and proven in a replica of the rig's environment before the rerun passed.

F · Saves & continuity

Established

Versioned saves

Only the save module writes the game state, into the platform's standard per-user location. The format is versioned (v13 on the default branch since the 2026-11-02 integration); every layout change in the project's history shipped with a migration, and fixtures spanning versions 4 through 13 still load in the suite. Integrity is checked on load; a save that fails its checks is rejected rather than half-applied.

Solved — FND-002, fixed 2026-11-02 (PR #24; issue #15 closed)

Continuation after reload

A reloaded game continued slightly differently from one that was never interrupted — measured, not anecdotal: the divergence began at the first step after reload, and the first differing byte sat in the economy's double-precision region. A private random stream was eliminated experimentally as the sole cause; the cause was then attributed: the per-minute weather was computed from the fraction of the minute, so the moment of a reload shifted the weather the game continued under. The fix evaluates each minute's weather at the minute's start, and a reloaded game is now byte-identical to one that never stopped. The hunt is worked case FND-002 on the mathematics page.

Not implemented

Tutorial · mod support · live map-data fetching

Named plainly so nobody mistakes them for features. The tutorial's underlying problem is addressed by the roadmap's onboarding phase; mod support and runtime map fetching are open design questions (see the evidence page), not silent decisions.

G · The reckoning — every system audited (2026-11-03)

On 2026-11-03 every major system was audited end to end: the creator's own design notes checked against the code as it stands and against its mathematics, intended against implemented against observed. The ranking below is that audit's verdict, healthiest first. Bar length and colour encode the rank — the audit's point is that the spread is wide: the foundation is strong and the top of the traffic system was, until the rework, the weakest code in the game.

The five weakest points, in the audit's own order:

Weakest point 1

ATC had no brain

The controller was memoryless — it scanned aircraft by aircraft and reacted, with no plan and no sequence — and its separation rules disagreed with each other in three places. Departures could starve behind arrival streams. This is the rework's whole subject (section H).

Weakest point 2

Passenger load factor is a dice roll

How full a flight is comes from a random number plus one adjustment — not from the ticket price, the time of day, or the airport's reputation. Demand and pricing are also the Mathematics Lab's last uninstrumented calculation.

Weakest point 3

The money does not reconcile

A landing fee was credited to the airport's cash and booked as debt the carrier still owed — then clawed back if the carrier went bankrupt. One movement of money, two records of it.

Weakest point 4

Simulated depth that changes nothing

Six of the eight staff departments are modelled in detail and barely affect the game. The detail is real; the consequences are not.

Weakest point 5

The same thing modelled twice, disagreeing

The audit's recurring disease: three separation rules, four turnaround clocks, three demand systems, three fare formulas — parallel models of one thing, free to drift apart. Each unification is a fix with a single owner; the separation rules were the first to be unified (R1, below).

One smaller dishonesty, recorded because the audit records everything it finds: a fuel event in the game announces that upkeep rises 35% while the code applies 20%.

H · The ATC rework (owner direction, 2026-11-03)

The owner's verdict on the old controller — “We need to completely rework the ATC system, it's very crude” — became a five-step rebuild, each step merged only on a green rig lane and verified by the full battery after merging. All five steps are in; the rework is complete.

Step 0 · Merged — PR #44, battery green

DEF-007 — speeds on final, assigned by the controller

The foundation: aircraft on final approach now fly controller-assigned speeds, so spacing is managed instead of hoped for. The step's first rig lane failed on two faults in the new code — both were diagnosed, fixed, and proven in a replica of the rig's environment before the rerun passed.

Done

↓

Step 1 · Merged — PR #45

R1 — one separation law, everywhere

The three disagreeing separation rules became one: the wake-turbulence table now governs admission to a runway, speed metering, and the player's own clearances, with a single 4,500 m arrival-close rule throughout. The player's old shortcut — pay a $3,000 fine and land anyway — was replaced by refusal: the law is the law for the player too.

Done

↓

Step 2 · Merged — PR #46

R2 — a real departure queue

Departures now queue per runway, longest-waiting first — the direct remedy for the starvation classified in the issues pass (FND-008) — and an idle runway gets used when a queue outgrows its own. Recorded honestly: the queue's first design broke saved-game continuation, and it was redesigned around saved state before it merged.

Done

↓

Step 3 · Merged — PR #47

R3 — arrivals fly to a sequence

Each arrival now owns a planned threshold time, spaced behind the aircraft ahead of it; the speeds that hold the sequence are assigned the DEF-007 way. The plan proposes — and the distance law vetoes: an assigned speed that would break the separation law is never issued.

Done

↓

Steps 4–5 · Merged 2026-11-03 — the rework is complete

R4 — ground separation · R5 — separation, measured

R4 scales ground separation by aircraft size (a gentle grade — 15/20/25/40 metres by class) and puts the deadlock breakers on the record: when the ground system has to break a jam by hand, Development Mode now says so. Its first test lane failed honestly and the diagnosis improved the game beyond the step itself: a departure queue could stand inside its own runway's edge and be refused clearance by itself, forever — a runway's own queue no longer counts as occupying it. R5 measures achieved separation live in Development Mode and closes the Mathematics Lab's separation-minima entry: the first Detroit sample recorded 7 successive pairs, none below the law, the tightest 3.3 times the required minimum — spacing there is set by how long a landing occupies the runway, not by the wake table. R4 merged as PR #48 (post-merge battery 41733631618, all six green); R5 as PR #49 (battery 41740576526, all six green). With step 5 the rework is complete.

Done
