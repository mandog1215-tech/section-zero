AIRPORTSIM — FIELD NOTES

Systems You Can Play With

AirportSim's machinery, described the way a player actually meets it: as decisions, temptations and consequences wearing overalls.

Contracts, both kinds

Work arrives as offers. Passenger routes are the classic board; freight now appears there in its own right, in freighter classes, at a share that matches the freighters actually flying — one offer in six, earned by a fix that started as an investigation into why cargo was nearly invisible.

The ledgers

Behind every contract, an airline keeps its private accounts: the delay that was your fault, the promise that expired quietly, the fee that stung. The ledger is invisible right up until it isn't — until a renewal goes silent and a rival's logo appears on your departures board.

The placement law

No aircraft parks where its body would touch a building. The rule is geometric, absolute, and enforced at the moment of placement — it converted a thousand silent overlaps into honest refusals, and it is why the repaired stands can be trusted rather than merely admired.

The chronicle

The airport writes its own diary: diversions, emergencies, the contract that got away, the morning everything worked. Players read it for the story. The sharper reason to respect it is that the airlines' memories are built from the same cloth.
