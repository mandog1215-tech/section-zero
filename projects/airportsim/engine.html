AIRPORTSIM — FIELD NOTES

Notes from the Engine Room

The machinery under the airfield: plain C, a world that never touches the drawing board, and a change of foundations performed mid-flight.

A world, separate from its picture

The simulation files are forbidden from including the graphics library's headers. The airport therefore exists — lands, delays, defaults — with no window open at all. That separation is what lets the test batteries run whole airports headlessly, thousands of times, while nobody watches.

Why change foundations

SDL2 carried the prototype era honourably. SDL3 is where the studio now stands: LONGSHOT flies on it, its design bench was proven on it, and a two-game studio compounds every lesson when both games share one base.

How a transplant is survived

In slices, each small enough to prove on its own: compile clean, run the four lanes, compare the photographs. The game must be demonstrably itself on the far side of the swap — same airports, same behaviour, same pictures — or the swap is not finished, whatever the compiler thinks.
