The dream — in the owner's words

“Basically, an airport sandbox. With heavy tycoon and god game elements. Wanna divert a flight just for the hell of it? You can. Wanna play as realistically as possible as a pilot or ATC? You can do that. Want to manage the airport financially while everything is run automatically. You can do that.”

— Andrew, the owner, 2026-11-03. One world, many stances — sandbox, tycoon, god game — switchable at will; full realism available but never forced; automation is a legitimate way to play. This is the standard the current programme is judged against.

The programme today (updated 2026-11-10)

Development Mode is complete. The WP-09 programme grew the F3 overlay into a full diagnostic environment in eight increments — all merged, each verified by a full post-merge battery: visual inspection; the Mathematics Lab; the Test Lab (experiments in separate processes); the Performance Lab; the Replay Lab; investigation history (every control decision recorded with its reason); an integrity trio (save check, determinism lamp, evidence gaps); and the economy rollup, which records each day's money line by line and re-adds it against the day's profit. Development Mode is strictly read-only: it shows what tests establish, what remains untested, where behaviour first deviates, and possible mechanisms — it never invents a cause beyond recorded evidence. On its completion the build advanced to 0.6.0-alpha.2 “Threshold” (PR #39; master at d8bb3c8).

The issues pass is done. Every open item was closed or classified with its evidence on 2026-11-03: test processes that shared one save folder (fixed, PR #41); long airline names cut off in the Business window (fixed with a self-sizing column, PR #42); three memory-recovery paths that could crash (fixed, PR #43); three sibling sites judged guarded, no change; departures starving behind arrival streams, traced to runway assignment with no load term (classified — its remedy is the departure queue in the rework below); aircraft drawn over terminal roofs (a real draw-order defect; the fix is designed and scheduled with the visuals pass); jet bridges that do engage at all 135 Detroit gate stands but are nearly invisible (visuals pass); and display scaling, whose high-DPI half turned out to be fixed in the code long ago — the remaining question, the game's own presentation at interface scales above 100%, will be judged at the owner's hands-on test version. The day also enforced the owner's brand rule: no real commercial brand names in the game — the terminal inspector's flavour names were replaced with invented ones, and the test suite now fails permanently if a real brand appears in terminal text (PR #40). The owner has since extended the same rule to airline names: the generator can no longer produce a real carrier — six roots were replaced with invented names chosen to keep the roster's random stream bit-identical — and a blocklist test now guards the whole roster permanently. It landed as the opening change of the world update below (PR #51).

The reckoning is written. Every major system was audited — the creator's design notes checked against the code and its mathematics — and ranked, healthiest first: simulation core, weather, economy & finance, ground operations, construction & layout, airlines/routes/network, staff & roles, events & emergencies, passengers/demand/pricing, and last, air traffic control. The full audit, its five weakest points, and the ranking chart are on the systems page.

The ATC rework is complete. On the owner's direction — “We need to completely rework the ATC system, it's very crude” — the weakest system was rebuilt in five steps, all merged and verified by a full post-merge battery: controller-assigned speeds on final (PR #44), one separation law everywhere (PR #45), a real departure queue (PR #46), arrival sequencing (PR #47), and ground separation by aircraft size with separation measured live in Development Mode (PRs #48, #49). Detail on the systems page.

The continuous world is underway. The owner's direction, 2026-11-03: the airport's 9 km tile with its hard edge — never his original scope — becomes one continuous world you can scroll or fly across, airport to airport, with one fully simulated airport at a time and the rest living statistical lives until you arrive. The first three steps are merged (PRs #51, #52, #61–#64, #66 and #67; master at 1687670): a double-precision world frame (worst measured round-trip error 0.00001 m), a geography pack format with real terrain data (Detroit and Denver areas built from ETOPO 2022 and Natural Earth), and a Development Mode world inspector — landed with the full test suite green (163,643 checks, 0 failed) and every soak hash byte-identical to the pre-world game, so the world stands on the simulation exactly as verified. The second step opens the view itself: the camera leaves the 9 km fence for a 150 km envelope, and the real geography — the Detroit River, Lake Erie, neighbouring airfields — renders to the horizon, with the full post-merge test battery green. About — two scales, one simulation

Global scale: a living aviation network runs across a real-Earth map — 4,531 airports, flights airborne around the clock, day and night sweeping the map, weather systems and real METAR reports, incidents that close runways.

Airport scale: inside that world you own and operate one airport — gates, construction, money, airline deals — or step into the work itself: pilot, air traffic controller, passenger, ramp worker, gate agent, security, airport firefighter. Eight playable roles, one coherent simulation underneath all of them. It is not an idle tycoon with mini-games attached; the management game and the operational game are the same world seen from different seats.
