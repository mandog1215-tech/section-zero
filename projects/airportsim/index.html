The dream — in the owner's words

“Basically, an airport sandbox. With heavy tycoon and god game elements. Wanna divert a flight just for the hell of it? You can. Wanna play as realistically as possible as a pilot or ATC? You can do that. Want to manage the airport financially while everything is run automatically. You can do that.”

— Andrew, the owner, 2026-11-03. One world, many stances — sandbox, tycoon, god game — switchable at will; full realism available but never forced; automation is a legitimate way to play. This is the standard the current programme is judged against.

The programme today (updated 2026-11-10)

Development Mode is complete. The WP-09 programme grew the F3 overlay into a full diagnostic environment in eight increments — all merged, each verified by a full post-merge battery: visual inspection; the Mathematics Lab; the Test Lab (experiments in separate processes); the Performance Lab; the Replay Lab; investigation history (every control decision recorded with its reason); an integrity trio (save check, determinism lamp, evidence gaps); and the economy rollup, which records each day's money line by line and re-adds it against the day's profit. Development Mode is strictly read-only: it shows what tests establish, what remains untested, where behaviour first deviates, and possible mechanisms — it never invents a cause beyond recorded evidence. On its completion the build advanced to 0.6.0-alpha.2 “Threshold” (2026-11-03).

The issues pass is done. Every open item was closed or classified with its evidence on 2026-11-03: test processes that shared one save folder (fixed); long airline names cut off in the Business window (fixed with a self-sizing column); three memory-recovery paths that could crash (fixed); three sibling sites judged guarded, no change; departures starving behind arrival streams, traced to runway assignment with no load term (classified — its remedy is the departure queue in the rework below); aircraft drawn over terminal roofs (a real draw-order defect; the fix is designed and scheduled with the visuals pass); jet bridges that do engage at all 135 Detroit gate stands but are nearly invisible (visuals pass); and display scaling, whose high-DPI half turned out to be fixed in the code long ago — the remaining question, the game's own presentation at interface scales above 100%, will be judged at the owner's hands-on test version. The day also enforced the owner's brand rule: no real commercial brand names in the game — the terminal inspector's flavour names were replaced with invented ones, and the test suite now fails permanently if a real brand appears in terminal text. The owner has since extended the same rule to airline names: the generator can no longer produce a real carrier — six roots were replaced with invented names chosen to keep the roster's random stream bit-identical — and a blocklist test now guards the whole roster permanently. It landed as the opening change of the world update below.

The reckoning is written. Every major system was audited — the creator's design notes checked against the code and its mathematics — and ranked, healthiest first: simulation core, weather, economy & finance, ground operations, construction & layout, airlines/routes/network, staff & roles, events & emergencies, passengers/demand/pricing, and last, air traffic control. The full audit, its five weakest points, and the ranking chart are on the systems page.

The ATC rework is complete. On the owner's direction — “We need to completely rework the ATC system, it's very crude” — the weakest system was rebuilt in five steps, all merged and verified by a full post-merge battery: controller-assigned speeds on final, one separation law everywhere, a real departure queue, arrival sequencing, and ground separation by aircraft size with separation measured live in Development Mode. Detail on the systems page.

The continuous world landed. The owner's direction, 2026-11-03: the airport's 9 km tile with its hard edge — never his original scope — became one continuous world you can scroll or fly across, airport to airport, with one fully simulated airport at a time and the rest living statistical lives until you arrive. It was built in measured steps: a double-precision world frame (worst measured round-trip error 0.00001 m); arriving flights born with real identities — airline, aircraft, callsign, origin — entering on the real bearing from their real origin, and departing flights handed off into onward tracks instead of vanishing at the fence; the new traffic then judged against the pre-world baseline at a pass line frozen in writing before the sample ran — a firm pass on all nine measures. A Development Mode skeleton view strips the running simulation to its structure, and airfield markings paint stand and runway numbers at every airport. The geography was measured before it was promised: the full planet pack — 1.46 GB of terrain, land cover and shorelines against a 2.5–3.0 GB working budget — is wired into the game, and on 2026-11-06 the owner approved the far view and closed the geography set: the approved look is real geography, smooth and clean at distance. The clean-sheet save system landed on 2026-11-07 as HL-SAVE 1: a file of named, individually versioned sections, a loader that cannot crash, and a one-way importer for every older save. The interface overhaul's status board is merged; its in-game frame was in progress when the programme paused. Four world steps were still to come — a second airport spinning up, the background law for far airports, continuous pilot flight, the owner in the world — with the Pilot, Passenger and ATC roles growing inside them. They are banked with the rest of the road. The queue from here is on the roadmap.

The roster grew from 36 to 58 aircraft types, including the classic trijets (727-200, DC-10-30, MD-11) with their tail engines drawn as engines; liveries are generated per carrier, with the airline's logo on the tail fin and the forward fuselage, and every gate carries a branding board in the serving airline's colours. The close-up screenshot proof caught three real defects before close — a fuselage logo that never painted, tail branding hidden behind the trijets' centre engine, and trijets that could never appear in live traffic — and all three were fixed and re-proven in a fresh official screenshot set, inspected image by image. The master battery is fully green on the owner's test rig: 192,032 simulation checks, 0 failed in the run of 2026-11-08, the sanitiser lanes passing separately on the same run. Work now moves to the foundations for the 7.0 “Latitude” test copy: real airport layouts, collision, and ground markings.

Airports no longer have to wear a generated layout: a pack of 246 real airports, built from OpenStreetMap data, now loads each field's actual runways, taxiways and stands in place of the generated ones. The proof ran on the owner's test rig: a soak passed at all nine sampled airports across two simulated days each, Detroit's layout compared identical with and without the pack, and the layout screenshots were inspected image by image (twelve of the thirteen views captured; one close view timed out on the rig and is noted, not hidden). A bake-tool defect found on the way — a manifest bug that silently dropped the pack's last two airports — was fixed and the pack rebuilt complete. The catalogue keeps growing in the background beyond the proven 246. This evening the owner received a snapshot of the game as it stands for hands-on testing — a development snapshot, not a release. (On 2026-11-10 he ordered that snapshot deleted, unplayed, and ruled there would be no more interim copies: the next copy he takes is the finished 7.0.) Update, 2026-11-10 — delayed, then resumed. By the owner's decision the programme was parked this morning and its status changed to delayed; within the hour he resumed it, and development is active again — the shortest delay in the studio's short history, recorded because it happened. The last work before the pause was diagnostic: the collision finding — aircraft overlapping terminal roofs at some stands — was measured and traced to three stacked causes (stand geometry, a flat draw order, and aircraft drawn at 1.4× true size), with the fix designed. A complete overhaul design, re-founding the game's mathematics on four primitives, was finished and banked the same day. The road continues from exactly where it stopped.

Update, 2026-11-11 — collision closed; this is the active programme. The studio spent a night on the space game (see LONGSHOT), then the owner sent it back here. The collision fix — stand geometry repaired across all 26 baked layouts, an allocation law that refuses any stand whose true rectangle touches a building, and aircraft drawn at true scale in depth order — passed its full four-lane test battery and merged the same night (PR #116). Nose-penetrating stands fell from hundreds per aircraft type to nearly zero; the stand in the original report, Detroit A40, went from a 16.9 m overlap to a 39.7 m clearance.

About — two scales, one simulation

Global scale: a living aviation network runs across a real-Earth map — 4,531 airports, flights airborne around the clock, day and night sweeping the map, weather systems and real METAR reports, incidents that close runways.

Airport scale: inside that world you own and operate one airport — gates, construction, money, airline deals — or step into the work itself: pilot, air traffic controller, passenger, ramp worker, gate agent, security, airport firefighter. Eight playable roles, one coherent simulation underneath all of them. It is not an idle tycoon with mini-games attached; the management game and the operational game are the same world seen from different seats.

What is being built

The queue stands like this: the collision fix first (diagnosed, designed, under construction), then ground markings, three small investigations (pilot recentring, a remapped key, cargo scarcity in the demo world), a hardening of the game's randomness, the interface renewal, and finally the full replay proof and the 7.0 “Latitude” handover. The programme was delayed on the morning of 2026-11-10 and resumed within the hour; nothing in the queue changed, and a complete overhaul design sits banked beside it — designed, not started. What follows is how the work got here.

M1 — “Green gate & safe repository” — is complete and accepted (2026-11-01): a fresh clone of the default branch passed the strict gate, the repository trap was closed, and the adoption documentation was reconciled. Since then, the development line has completed the five-stage Development Mode workstream and the performance-baseline work package.

The integration (2026-11-02) — complete. The owner authorized a dependency-ordered sequence of eleven packages. All eleven are merged to the default branch, each verified after its merge: the save-persistence fixes (OwnerBook attribution, save format v11; FleetBook accumulators, v12; the simulation-clock widening, v13), the cargo save/reload audit, the whole-world reproducibility tests, the save-loader fuzz harness, the adoption framework documents, the test tooling (CI now runs four jobs), and a three-fix package (deterministic test worlds for the game binary, a top-bar overlap fix, and a latitude-conversion undefined-behaviour fix). The screenshot-capture reliability package (PR #9) merged after its checks found a Windows build failure in its own capture code — a one-line fix, made and re-verified the same day. The test-seeding package (PR #11) merged as the recorded resolution: its seeding turned out to be already covered by the three-fix package. The Development Mode branch merged last, after two small fixes on its first trip through the full checks (a Windows clock call made portable; a memory leak in a test, caught by the memory-safety run), both fixed and re-verified before merging. The final full gate on the integrated tree passed — 162,701 simulation checks and 9,676 interface checks, zero failed, end-to-end flows green, and all four CI jobs passing on the final code. No pull requests remain open. The integrated build is named 0.6.0-alpha.1 “Threshold” — the project's first Alpha and its first codename, approved by the owner on 2026-11-02; the code's version string then read 0.6.0-alpha.1. The name is an internal development identity, not a release: no tag or public package exists for it, and the last public build remains 0.5.3. Update, 2026-11-03: with Development Mode complete, the build advanced to 0.6.0-alpha.2 “Threshold”, codename kept — see “The programme today” above.

The intermittent failure that shadowed the sequence — an unseeded test world making the interface test nondeterministic — is fixed for determinism. A related cosmetic defect was tracked as issue #14 (the Business window truncating long airline names); it was fixed on 2026-11-03 with a self-sizing column (PR #42).

What came next, in order — all of it now done: the save/reload continuation investigation (issue #15) ran first, as the owner directed, and was solved — a minute's weather was computed from the fraction of the minute, so a reload quietly changed the weather; the fix made a reloaded game byte-identical to one that never stopped (PR #24). The Windows verification harness followed, and then moved — with every other check — to a self-hosted rig on the owner's PC, where a native Windows battery ran on every merge through the rig era. (The checks have since moved for good: since 2026-11-10 they run on the four GitHub-hosted Linux lanes, each run approved by the owner, and the game is Linux-only — he plays it through Ubuntu on WSL.) WP-09 then grew Development Mode into the full diagnostic environment described above. Where the programme stands now is summarized in the programme today; the forward queue is on the roadmap. M1's original criteria remain:

  • A clean clone of the default branch passes the strict release gate
  • Test counts at or above the baseline (162,315 / 9,676), zero failures
  • The automated pipeline passes on the same commit
  • The source diff contains only the approved one-line gate fix (+ justified kin)
  • The five documentation discrepancies are reconciled
  • The default-branch problem is closed by the owner's approved route

All eight were satisfied with evidence — the details live on the engineering & testing page. The register has moved since: of the three Beta gates, two were satisfied in their era — native Windows verification (the owner's PC was the test rig, and a native Windows battery ran on every merge; K3 — the gate was retired on 2026-11-10 when the game went Linux-only) and the save/reload fix (FND-002, PR #24). The owner's 2026-11-02 hands-on with a pre-adoption Windows build (0.5.4, by the original author) — about an hour at Detroit, launched and played correctly, saves not tested — stands as the provisional gameplay record for that older build. The third gate, the full 14-scenario × 3-seed end-to-end matrix rerun (K6), remains open deliberately: it is sequenced last, immediately before the Beta decision, which is the owner's.
